How I work with AI

I use Claude, a language model made by Anthropic, in some of my work. This page says where, and what it touched.

Before title pages, a book’s identity sat at its end, in a note called the colophon: what the book was, who made it, where, and when. Medieval scribes used it to speak for themselves. Tres digiti scribunt totum corpusque laborat. “Three fingers write, the whole body labours.” Every page on this site ends with a colophon.

Four lines

Witnessed
Where the piece came from: the book, the film, the street, the night.
Made by hand
Whose sentences, cuts and choices these are.
Generated
Where a machine’s words or work went in, and where they sit now.
Checked
Which facts were held up against their sources, and by whom.

Under the four lines goes my mark: the alphabet, written by hand in one line. I have been writing it for twenty years.

Three tests

A colophon can be staged. Mine have to pass three tests.

Checkable
It points to records someone else could inspect.
Costly
It includes at least one line I’d rather leave out.
Plain
It reads like a receipt.

Five receipts from one page

My résumé, October 2026.

  1. A number

    It says I carried “400+ BFA Animation students a year from 2016 to 2019, about 600 in all.” The yearly figure is mine. The total is an estimate I asked Claude to make from it, which is why it says “about.”

  2. A date

    An early draft said I had advised for just under five years. My CV from the time said June 2016 to May 2019. The CV won.

  3. A mark

    The mark at the foot of the page is my alphabet line, photographed. Claude turned the photo right side up, removed the paper around the strokes and cleared stray specks. The strokes are as I wrote them.

  4. A page

    I supplied the facts and checked each one. Claude drafted the sentences. The colophon printed on the résumé says so: “Facts gathered and checked by Jasper Palmer; written with Claude (Anthropic), October 2026.”

  5. A choice

    Claude laid out four places the mark could go. I chose the foot of the page, where printers put theirs.
